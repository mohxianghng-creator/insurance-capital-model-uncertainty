Read first. Stylised, public industry data; not a real insurer’s capital, not regulatory capital. Backtests cannot validate these numbers (11 years). Compare the cost and conservatism of each rule. Five synthetic worlds do not establish a recommended rule for a real portfolio; some simulation outputs exceed the numerical grid.

Models behind the rules

Plug-in = MLE point estimate. Predictive = capital from the mixture over parameter uncertainty. Range = 5–95% parameter interval, retaining unresolved draws above the grid. Bootstrap and semi-Bayesian intervals have different interpretations; neither is a guaranteed bound on total uncertainty. See paper §6.6.

Current-engine validation

Synthetic worlds measure behaviour where the true quantile is known. Monte Carlo intervals describe experiment precision; they do not validate an actual insurer.

Precision limit. Capital-rule cost comparisons use B=80 and remain exploratory. Fixed-sample B=240/B=960 sensitivity is documented in the evidence report. The finite-treaty analysis uses a separate deterministic calculation.

These experiments use Poisson frequency, ΔAIC ≤ 10 and bootstrap predictives; the frequency, cutoff and parameter-method controls above apply to the empirical capital cards. Loss is dimensionless relative capital error, weighted by the shortfall/excess cost ratio above. Displayed means are lower bounds when censored outputs remain. Estimation shortfalls differ from annual loss breaches. B=80/B=240 precision diagnostics are in the evidence report.

Finite-layer actions under a resource budget

A 100 xs 20 layer pays up to 100m DKK above 20m per claim. Losses above exhaustion return to the insurer. All pricing assumptions are illustrative.

Feasibility requires net VaR plus upfront premium to fit the budget in all nine plausible risk views. The conditional minimax-regret comparison uses the common feasible set. Credit risk, recovery delays, aggregate limits and reinstatements are excluded. Obtain actual quotations and terms before acting.
